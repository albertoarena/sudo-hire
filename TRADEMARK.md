# Trademark and brand policy

Sudo Hire is free software, released under the [MIT license](LICENSE). This page
is about the things the license does not cover: the project name and the brand
around it.

Nothing on this page takes away any right the MIT license gives you over the code.

## What the MIT license covers

The MIT license covers the **source code** of Sudo Hire. You may use it, copy it,
change it, merge it into your own work, publish it and sell it, for free. The one
condition: keep the copyright notice and the license text with it.

## What this policy covers

The MIT license is a copyright license. It does not grant any right to use the
project's name or brand. Those are separate, and they are:

- The name **Sudo Hire**, when used for job search, hiring or developer tooling.
- The tagline **`$ sudo hire --remote-from here`**, and the same line with another
  place, such as `--remote-from italy`, used as a slogan.
- **All the art made for the project**, wherever it appears: the app icon, the menu
  bar mark, logos, cover and social images. This covers every size and color
  variant, still or animated. That includes the copies the app ships with.
- The domain **sudohire.dev**, and the design and layout of the site on it.

These identify this project. They are how people know that a thing came from here,
and they are not part of the MIT grant.

The word `sudo` on its own is not covered. It is the name of a Unix command, and
nobody owns it.

## What you can do without asking

You do not need permission for any of this, and you are welcome to it:

- Say that your project uses, supports, works with, or was inspired by Sudo Hire.
- Link to the repository or to [sudohire.dev](https://sudohire.dev).
- Write articles, reviews, tutorials, videos or talks about Sudo Hire, including
  paid ones, and use the name to say what they are about.
- Use the unmodified art, or a screenshot of the app, in an article, a review or a
  talk about Sudo Hire. Add a link back to the project.
- **Fork the repository on GitHub to contribute.** Nothing on this page applies to
  that. See "Contributing" below.
- Fork the code and change it for your own use, as the license allows.

## What needs permission

Please do not do any of this without asking first:

- Give **your own** app, product, domain, social account or company a name that
  people could read as this project or its official version. That
  includes `sudo-hire-*`, `*-sudo-hire` and "Sudo Hire" followed by another word,
  used as the product's own name. **A GitHub fork made to contribute is not
  covered**: it keeps this project's name, and it should.
- Use the tagline as the slogan of your own product, or in your own marketing.
- Use the app icon, the menu bar mark or other art from this project as the icon,
  logo or hero image of your product. This also applies to a changed
  version of the art. An app icon in particular is how people tell apps apart in the Dock, in
  Finder and in their menu bar.
- Say or imply that your work is official, endorsed, approved, affiliated with, or
  built by the Sudo Hire project, when it is not.
- Register a trademark, domain, bundle identifier or app store name containing
  "Sudo Hire", or a name close enough to be confused with it.
- Sell merchandise carrying the name, the art or the tagline.

## Rebuilds and reimplementations

You are free to build your own job finder, menu bar job alert or remote eligibility
checker. The idea is not owned by anyone, and a good one is welcome.

If you build your own, give it your own name, your own icon and your own look. Do not
launch it as a "Sudo Hire clone". Do not put "sudo-hire" or "sudohire" in its
address, and do not carry over the tagline or the art. You may of course say, in the
description, that Sudo Hire inspired it, and a link back is appreciated.

The reason is not ownership of the idea. A person who installs your app and thinks
it is this one will ask this project for support and report its bugs here. That
person will also judge Sudo Hire by software that Sudo Hire did not write.

## Contributing: nothing here applies

If you are forking the repository to fix a bug, add a feature, add a source or open
a pull request, **stop reading. This page is not about you.**

Fork it, keep the name, keep the app id and the icon exactly as they are, push your
branch, open the PR. Your fork is meant to look like Sudo Hire, because it is Sudo
Hire. That needs no permission from anyone. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).

The section below is about something different: publishing your version as a separate
app that people install instead of this one.

## Publishing your own distribution

This is a hard fork, not a contribution. You build and distribute your own version,
under your own name, for other people to install. The MIT license
lets you, and there is nothing wrong with it.

When you do:

- Give the app a different name, and change its bundle identifier
  (`NATIVEPHP_APP_ID`, `dev.sudohire.app` here) so macOS treats it as a separate
  app.
- Replace the app icon and the menu bar mark with your own.
- Keep the copyright notice and the license text, as the license requires.
- Keep the source terms: every job keeps its source name, its original link and its
  original apply link. The job boards set these terms, not this project.
- Say in the README that it is a fork of Sudo Hire, and link back here.
- Do not use the Sudo Hire name, art or tagline as the fork's own brand.

**The test is whether someone can install your version by mistake**, thinking it is
this one. A pull request cannot. A DMG with this icon, or an app store listing with
this name, can.
